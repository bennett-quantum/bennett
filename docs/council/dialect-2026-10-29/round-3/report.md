## Regressions reported

- **[sol]** TDL-7 previously required non-fault loop flags cleaned before invocation return. The diff removes them from that requirement and adds the TDL-8 program-end exemption in TDL-10. Although the earlier loop adjoint already needed repair, this previously correct cleanup obligation has been replaced by a rule that dephases successful identity actions (SOL-R3-01).
- **[codex]** TDL-7/TDL-10 now exempt ordinary Co-loop controls from the previous clean-workspace requirement. For a preserved-count identity loop, cleanup was possible and required by that requirement; the new mandatory program-end retention records the count and destroys later interference. This is R3-CODEX-01, not a request for a proof format.

## Blocking issues

- **[sol] SOL-R3-01** (§10 TDL-7 and TDL-10; §18.2; Appendix A A11; core/S0): The BD3-2 replacement makes loop flags and per-iteration conditions "declared control outputs with TDL-8 lifetime: leak-exempt, unmeasured until program end". This drops the previously correct obligation to clean control workspace before action return. Consider a successful, sufficiently budgeted action B: for (i=0; i<n; ++i) X(z), with n supported only on 0 and 2 and z initially 0. Its net source action is identity on this domain. The two nontrivial continuation flags are 00 on n=0 and 11 on n=2. Retention produces (|n=0,z=0,00>+|n=2,z=0,11>)/sqrt(2), rather than (|0,0>+|2,0>)/sqrt(2) tensor clean workspace. This is observable without direct access to arena wires: prepare arena n=2 under a |+> source control c, run B under a separate basis-one control, undo the preparation of n, then H(c) and measure c. Retained flags give P(c=1)=1/2; the promised unitary gives 0. There is no fault or source measurement in B. An independent checked ISA-0.8 run-action probe of the reduced sequence H(n[1]); CX(n[1],c0); CX(n[1],c1); CX(c0,z); CX(c1,z); H(n[1]) returns four amplitudes of magnitude 1/2, hence P(n[1]=1)=1/2. Uncomputing c1,c0 before the final H returns only basis tuple 00000 with amplitude 1. The new compute witness does clean its flags, but does not test successful forward return. NS §4.2 and ISA §§5.3/6.2 require control workspace restored; an implicit program-end measurement channel narrows that contract. *Fix:* In TDL-7/10 and §18.2 state: "Conditions, continuation flags and preserved trajectory records of a successful Co loop are control workspace. They MUST be uncomputed within their dependency-validity windows and before the enclosing successful action returns; declaring them compiler outputs does not exempt them. For conditions reconstructed from preserved inputs, compute bounded conditions/flags out of place, apply the controlled bodies, then reverse their exact computing sequences after the last dependent use. For overwritten trajectory inputs, retain/reconstruct the required bounded versions and clean the control computation while those versions are available, restoring the trajectory workspace as well. The same obligation applies to standalone adj and compute. Genuine measurement records and actual fault outputs keep their existing separate lifetimes." Keep the structured inverse rule, but remove the blanket program-end control-output exemption. Operations that are not bijective on their declared interface use the existing out-of-place/compute distinction, not a hidden measurement channel; do not reject Co loops as a class. Add the witness above: all codes 0, z=0, P(c=1)=0, and all loop flags/trajectory workspace clean at return.
